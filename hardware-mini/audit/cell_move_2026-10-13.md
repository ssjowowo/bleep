# Audit: cell moved under the keys (2026-10-13), mini28 + mini24

Independent audit of the redesign that moved the Li-Po cell from under the screen to under the key area (below the ESP32 module) on both mini remotes. Everything below was re-derived from the files. The project was not modified.

**Method.** Two inputs:
- the current tree;
- the pre-change archive `~/bleep-backups/hardware-mini-2026-10-13-before-battery-move.tgz`, extracted to `~/.cache/bleep-audit-old/x1/`.

Work products are in `~/.cache/bleep-audit/`:
- `cur/`: a copy of the tree, used to run `layout.py`, OpenSCAD and DRC without touching the project.
- `*_drc.rpt`, `*_drc_refill.rpt`: KiCad 10.0.6 DRC, `--severity-all`, on the saved boards and after a zone refill.
- `*.new.json`, `*.old.json`: pcbnew dumps of every footprint, pad and net, track, via and zone island, current and archived.
- `*_layout.txt`: fit-check output.
- `scad/results.txt`: OpenSCAD interference runs.
- `regen/`: Gerbers regenerated from the current boards.
- `scripts/`: the analysis scripts.

## Verdict

No Critical findings. The electrical change is clean on both boards:
- the netlists are unchanged, pad for pad;
- the reverse-polarity FET is wired correctly and matches the silk "+";
- GND is continuous everywhere;
- DRC is 0/0;
- the fab files exactly match the boards.

The mechanical design works and its own checks pass. It has one Major issue: the charger now sits over the cell, which was never assessed. The Minor findings are mostly tolerance margins and robustness details. The balance claim holds.

## Major

### MA1 (both): the 1 W charger U5 now sits over the cell, which has no temperature sensing, and nobody assessed it

**Where U5 sits** (`*.new.json`):

| | U5 (TP4056, ESOP-8, rot 90) | the cell | overlap |
|---|---|---|---|
| mini28 | (48.30, 142.23), body ≈ x 45.9–50.7, y 140.3–144.2 | x 6.2–48.2, y 105.63–156.63 | ≈ 2.4 mm over the cell's right edge |
| mini24 | (14.20, 103.66) | x 3.46–43.46, y 91.51–141.51 | entirely over the cell, 12 mm below its top end |

**How far it was before** (archive `params.scad`):
- mini28: the cell's centre was at y 36.95, so U5 was ≈ 72 mm from the cell.
- mini24: the cell's centre was at y 32.2, so U5 was ≈ 46 mm away.

**Why it matters:**
- U5's exposed pad has 3–4 thermal vias into the bottom pour, which faces the deck and the cell.
- The separation is only the board (0.8), the PETG deck (0.8 / 0.6) and the 0.5 mm swell gap.
- U5 dissipates up to ≈ 1.0 W at the start of a charge (mini28 README §6; mini24 README §5) and ≈ 0.75 W typically.
- TEMP (pin 1) is tied to GND on both boards (`U5.1 = GND`), so nothing measures the cell's temperature.
- A rough estimate puts the pouch surface under U5 at ≈ 15–20 K above ambient during constant-current charging, inside a closed shell. That is at or near the usual 45 °C charge limit on a warm day.
- Neither README's risk list, nor §12/§14, nor DESIGN.md's last section mentions it.

**Fix:**
1. Add a risk entry and a first-build test: a thermocouple on the pouch under U5, charging at 500 mA from ≈ 3.0 V with the shell closed.
2. Cheap mitigation that keeps the copper: raise R16 from 2.4 k to 3.3 k (≈ 360 mA, 0.24 C; a 0603 value swap in JLC's top-side BOM, same footprint). This cuts U5's dissipation by ≈ 30 %, and charging still takes < 5 h.
3. The firmware can't limit charging: the TP4056 has no enable pin on these boards. The only levers are the PROG resistor or an NTC. A future board could take a 10 k NTC on TEMP, or put U5 off the cell.

## Minor

### MI1 (mini28): board-screw heads clamp the PETG deck on a 0.45 mm ring, with no washer
- C357393 (the M2×4 in the BOM) has a Ø3.3 head; mini24 BOM: "head only Ø3.3: always with the washer below".
- The deck's holes are Ø2.4 (`SCREW_CLR_D`), so the head bears on a 0.45 mm-wide PETG annulus (≈ 4 mm²). At ordinary M2 preload (a few hundred N) that is well past PETG's compressive yield.
- The PETG will crush and creep, so the board-to-post joint (deck in series) loosens over time.
- Before the change the head sat on FR-4, with a Kapton disc.
- mini24 solved the same problem with DIN 125 washers (Ø5 × 0.3).
- **Fix:** add an M2 washer under each of the 3 board screws. Add them to the BOM, SHOPPING.md and README §4 step 9. Check that the washer's 0.3 mm still fits: the head room is 1.6 mm, and the gap above the plate is 5.3 mm. Alternatively, shrink the deck holes to Ø2.2.

### MI2 (both): a cell pushed into a bottom corner touches the plate's lip; the "0.57–0.9 mm" corner margins only hold for a centred cell
Computed from `layout.py` (`scripts/geo.py`):
- **mini28:** nominal corner (6.20, 156.63) has 0.90 mm. A cell slid to a rail and against the standoffs has its corner at (5.50, 157.33), giving **−0.06 mm** (contact).
- **mini24:** nominal (3.46, 141.51) has 0.69 mm, and 0.57 when pushed down. Pushed down and against a rail, the corner is at (2.85, 142.36), giving **−0.035 mm**. The rails end at y 140.51, before the curve.

The lip is only 1.5 mm tall and pouch corners are rounded, so this means a light touch, not damage. Still, the README claims "≥ 0.7 mm inside the plate's lip at its corners" (mini28 §1 fit checks) and "0.69 / 0.57" (mini24 §3), and with a lip printed ~0.1–0.2 mm fat the touch becomes a bind.

**Fix:** pick one of:
- mini24: extend the cell rails down to the bottom stops; mini28: lengthen the inboard face of the deck rails;
- add a 45° chamfer to the lip's inner corner;
- move the bottom stops/standoffs 0.3 mm up.

Also correct the margin text to "for a centred cell".

### MI3 (mini28): the reset slot leaves the cell's long edge exposed
- The pill-shaped slot spans x 49.0–51.8, y 105.48–111.88 (`EN_SLOT`). It is cut through the plate, the lip and the right deck rail, which spans x 48.9–52.25, up to z 8.6 (`remote.scad` back_plate()).
- That leaves a 0.1 mm "wall" on the cell side, which won't print.
- The cell's right edge is 0.8 mm away (x 48.2), and the slot overlaps the cell's length (y 105.6–156.6).
- Metal tweezers used to short EN–GND (README §4 step 4) can slide sideways onto the pouch edge.
- mini24's slot sits beside the plug, away from the cell, so it doesn't have this problem.
- **Fix:** keep a ≥ 0.8 mm wall on the cell side: narrow the slot to x 49.7–51.8, or move the rail cut-out 0.7 mm right. Or add a sentence to step 4 saying to use an insulated probe.

### MI4 (mini28): the deck and the right deck rail reach under the antenna keep-out
- The deck starts at y 101.78 and spans x 2.4–52.0 (`DECK_MAIN`). Its only cut-outs are USB-C and EN.
- So 0.8 mm of PETG lies against the board under the keep-out's tail (x 44.5–52, y 101.78–104.68).
- The 3.35 × 5.3 mm right rail starts at y 101.98, 0.8 mm below the antenna section's bottom corner.
- mini24 deliberately notches both: `DECK_RELIEFS[0]`, and rails from keep-out + 1.0.
- PETG near the antenna detunes it a little, and mini28's antenna already has only 0.6 mm to the wall (§8 risk 9).
- **Fix:** copy mini24. Notch the deck at x ≥ 44.5, y < 105.2, and start the right deck rail at y ≥ 105.7, above the reset-slot cut that is already there.

### MI5 (mini24): the deck has 0.25 mm per side to the shell walls, and it drops in after the board
- `deck2d()` is `outline2d(-WALL - 0.25)`. The deck is 42.82 mm wide in a 43.32 mm cavity.
- `deck_path` passes only because it tests with +0.1, which leaves 0.15.
- A 3-layer PETG print is almost all first layer, and elephant foot alone is 0.1–0.2 mm per side. Add the shell's own wall tolerance and the deck may not drop in.
- **Fix:** take the deck outline in to 0.45 mm from the walls (`outline2d(-WALL - 0.45)`). Then widen the cell rails inboard by 0.2 mm (x 2.05–3.05), so the deck edge still sits ≥ 0.6 mm on each rail. The cell's side play drops from 0.61 to 0.41 mm. Or keep the geometry and tell the user to turn on elephant-foot compensation and deburr the deck's edges.

### MI6 (mini24): BOM gives the mic gasket as OD 2.3 / ID **1.8**; the design is OD 2.3 / ID **1.1**
- Edited today: the archive had the even older "OD 2.0 / ID 0.8".
- Where: `mainboard/fab/mainboard-BOM.csv` foam line, from `gen_mainboard.py:1524`.
- The design value: `layout.py:393` gives `MIC_GASKET = (2.3, 1.1, 0.75)`, and README §6 step 5 says OD 2.3 / ID 1.1 with Ø1.1 / Ø2.3 punches.
- A 1.8 ID ring would have a 0.25 mm wall and wouldn't seal.
- **Fix:** change line 1524 to "OD 2.3 / ID 1.1" and regenerate the BOM (and SHOPPING if it copies it).

### MI7 (both): haptics moved under the screen, away from the hand and the screws; on mini24 the motor sits 15 mm from the accelerometer
**Distance from the motor to the nearest screw:**

| | before | now |
|---|---|---|
| mini28 | ≈ 18 mm (motor at 7.4, 114.4) | ≈ 24 mm (motor at 10.9, 66.0; screw H1 at 6.3, 90.03) |
| mini24 | 11.7 mm (motor at 9.6, 81.0) | ≈ 30 mm (motor at 9.6, 39.97; screw H1 at 5.4, 69.61) |

- **Coupling:** the motor still reaches the shell through the board, then the plate's rails and pillars, and through the foam and panel. So it still couples, but more diffusely, and farther from where the hand holds the remote (the balance point is now ≈ 90 mm).
- **Accelerometer:** on mini24, U4 (MMA8452Q) is at (8.2, 24.77), 15.3 mm from the motor, so lift-to-wake may trigger on haptic pulses. mini28's U4 is 37 mm away.
- **Fix:**
  - mini24 README §7: mask or ignore accelerometer interrupts while the DRV2605L plays.
  - Both: try the haptic strength on the first build. If it is weak, add a plate pillar under the motor's can (it is on the underside, with 3.4 mm of room under it) so the motor is clamped to the back plate.

### MI8 (both): key feel depends on rail-to-deck tolerances, and mini24 lost its mid-span posts
- The rails, deck and board touch with zero designed interference: rail top = `Z_DECK_BOT`.
- **mini24:** the board is now held only at H1 (y 69.6) and the bottom bosses (y 151), an 81 mm span. H2/H3 at y 107.8 are gone.
  - Rails printed 0.1–0.2 mm proud bow the D-pad area toward the caps. The stem gap is 0.1 mm and the TS-1187A's travel is ≈ 0.25 mm, so that can pre-press keys.
  - Rails printed short leave the board alone over the span. A 2 N press on 0.8 mm FR-4 over 81 mm bows it ≈ 0.6 mm before the deck lands, which feels mushy.
- **mini28** keeps three clamped points on the deck, so it is less sensitive.
- Thin (0.6–0.8 mm) PETG plates also tend to curl at their corners.
- **Fix:**
  - Add to README §8/§12 risks.
  - Give `fit_test`-style guidance: print the deck with a brim at 100 % infill and check it lies flat. Check the rails against the deck with a straight edge.
  - Consider rails 0.1 mm short plus a 0.2 mm foam strip on the rail tops, which gives a defined preload.

### MI9 (both): a swelling cell now pushes on the key switches
- Before, a swelling cell (beyond the 0.5 mm gap) pushed the board area under the LCD, which sits on foam.
- Now it pushes the deck and board under the switches. The stem gaps are 0.15 (mini28) and 0.1 (mini24), so a cell swollen ≈ 0.6 mm makes keys self-actuate.
- That is a useful early warning, but nothing documents it.
- **Fix:** add to the risks and the firmware notes: a key stuck down for a long time plus high cell age means check the cell for swelling, and replace it.

### MI10 (both): the cell's length margin depends on the unverified PCM length
- mini28: the bay is 52.4 mm, for cells up to 51.2. mini24: 51.7 mm, for cells up to 50.5.
- research/batteries.md §1: "Ampul never says whether L includes the PCM … 51 + leads ≈ 55".
- Both READMEs list this as a risk (mini28 §8 risk 3; mini24 §12 risk 7), but neither says how far a rebuild can stretch:
  - mini28: `BLEEP_BATT` can lengthen the cell up to ≈ 53.4 mm. Beyond that, `check()` fails "less than 2 mm from the plug to the cell's lead end". This is computed, with the bottom held at y 156.63.
  - mini24: there is no `BLEEP_BATT` override. The bay can only grow ≈ 0.6 mm before the deck bosses (y 143.0).
- **Fix:** add these limits to both READMEs, and say plainly: buy and measure one cell before printing the back plate. On mini24 also before the deck.

### MI11 (mini24): the plate screws clamp a 0.7 mm PETG counterbore floor
- The plate is 1.5 mm, the standoff 1.0 mm, and the counterbore 1.8 mm deep. That leaves 0.7 mm of PETG under the Ø3.3 head, on a 0.45 mm-wide annulus against the deck-boss insert.
- Before, the M2×8 went into a full-height standoff, with several mm of PETG under the head.
- The brass insert backs the floor, so the head shouldn't pull through. But the floor is crushed and creeps if over-tightened, and these are the battery-door screws, removed at every cell change.
- The deck-boss inserts sit in a boss with a 1.1 mm cap (0.5 boss + 0.6 deck) over a 4.0 mm hole. Pressing the insert too far raises a bump on the board side of the deck.
- **Fix:**
  - Add a Ø5 washer under the plate screws too, with the counterbore at Ø5.4, or raise `PLATE_BOSS_H` to 1.5 and shorten the deck boss.
  - Add to README step 4: press the deck inserts flush and no deeper, with the deck face-down on a flat surface.

## Nit

1. **mini28 README:** §1 stack table says the plate-screw "heads 3.0 mm deep in counterbores", but §1 "Back plate" says "(2.2 mm below the back face)". With `PLATE_HEAD_RECESS` 3.0 and a 1.6 mm pan head, the head is ≈ 1.4 mm below the face. **Fix:** "≈ 1.4 mm below the back face".
2. **mini28 README §12:** "the remote gets ≈ 7 g lighter (≈ 126 g)". From the STL volumes as solid PETG, the plate gained 2.1 g, the deck adds 3.1 g, the shell 1.0 g, and the cell loses 10 g, for ≈ −4 g. **Fix:** "≈ 3–4 g lighter".
3. **Lead-length guidance is short if the leads leave the far corner.** The wires leave at one corner (research/batteries.md). From the right corner of the mini28 cell to J4 is ≈ 28 mm, + 10 mm = 38, outside README §4 step 5's "≈ 15–30 mm". From the right corner of the mini24 cell to J2 is ≈ 33 mm, over §3/§6's "≈ 30 mm". **Fix:** "measure the real path + 10 mm; up to ≈ 40 mm if the leads leave the far corner".
4. **DESIGN.md "Hand assembly rules":** the underside rule still lists only "the ESP32 module, the battery connector, the motor's wires, the EN/GND pads". Q4 + R24 / R28 are on the underside of both boards now. mini28 §7 records it as a deviation; mini24 doesn't.
5. **DESIGN.md "Mechanical rules":** "H ≈ 5.2 + panel + cell with v1's stack" no longer holds with the deck: mini28 H = 10.5 + cell, mini24 H = 10.73 + cell. Also mini24's `layout.py` docstring says the 6 mm cells give "16.73". Fine, but DESIGN's formula is stale.
6. **Top-level README "Before ordering" items 4–5** still say "combine with the JLCPCB board + stencil order" and "Each README gives the reflow plan". That predates JLC Economic placing the top side (2026-10-11), not today's change, but it is still stale.
7. **mini24 README §8:** the list numbering still runs 1, 2, 3, 4b, 5, 4. This was the final verification's N3, still unfixed.
8. **mini28: the GND pour piece near (18.8, 99.0)**, the fab log's "no stitching spot". It is a 5.0 mm² F.Cu sliver (bbox 18.79–19.86 × 98.97–105.78) containing SW4 pad 1 (GND), a GND track and a via. KiCad connectivity confirms every GND item reaches the module's EPAD (`scripts/gnd.py`: none lost), so it is pad-fed, not floating. Harmless.
   - Related: several B.Cu pour fragments are tied by a single via. The largest is 7.7 × 13.2 mm (x 28.9–36.6, y 101.9–115.0, 18 mm²), just below the module. Similar fragments existed before and were accepted.
   - **Optional:** a second via at the far end of fragments longer than 10 mm.
9. **mini24 EN trace:** 46 mm of 0.15 mm F.Cu from C3 to TP1 (was 10 mm). It is a stub on the capacitor side of the RC, so the risk is low. No action needed beyond noting it.
10. **mini28 SHOPPING.md totals** say "(motor, screws, foam, washers…)", but mini28 has no washers. This becomes true if MI1 is adopted.
