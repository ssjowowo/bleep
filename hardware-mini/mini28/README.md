# Bleep mini28 — hardware (2.8" panel)

The 2.8" Bleep: v1's "Pebble" shell and key layout around a 2.8" IPS panel with glass touch, one main board of our own, a replaceable 1500 mAh cell under the keys (in the lower half, so the remote isn't top-heavy) that plugs in at its top end (reverse-polarity protected), a PDM microphone for push-to-talk, and a 32.768 kHz crystal. Built in small quantities: **JLCPCB places the top side** (Economic PCBA, top only) and **you solder the underside by hand** (the ESP32 module, the battery header, its reverse-polarity FET and resistor, the motor wires).

| | |
|---|---|
| Shell | **54.4 × 168.8 × 15.3 mm** (concept print: 54.4 × 167.8 × 14.5; 14.1 with the cell under the screen until 2026-10-13, §12) |
| Main board | 49.6 × 165.1 mm (incl. the USB-C tab), 0.8 mm, 2 layers, **green mask, lead-free HASL** (the only finish JLC's Economic PCBA offers at 0.8 mm). KiCad DRC: **0 violations, 0 unconnected** (`--severity-all`, with JLCPCB's silk minimums as rules) |
| Display | EastRising ER-TFT028A2-4 (ILI9341, IPS, 240 × 320, **4-wire SPI**) + ER-TPC028-4.1 glass CTP (FT6206). 50-pin FPC into J1, separate 6-pin touch tail into J2 |
| ESP32 | ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-**N8R2** on the underside, antenna at the right edge (seen from the front) |
| Battery | **Ampul AM7240 "484251": 4.8 × 42 × 51 mm, 1500 mAh**, protected, **under the keys** (below the module) on a printed deck, plugs into a PicoBlade header (J4) on the underside at its top end |
| New vs v1 | SPI panel, separate touch ZIF, PDM mic (MSM261DHT006) under a port hole, 32 kHz crystal, battery connector + reverse-polarity FET, USB ESD/TVS, TX debug pad, top side assembled by JLC (Economic), underside by hand |

Changes after the independent audit (`../audit/mini28_electrical.md`, `../audit/mini28_mechanical.md`) are listed in §9.

Everything is generated from [`layout.py`](layout.py) (mechanics, positions, GPIO map, fit checks):

```bash
./build.sh layout      # enclosure/params.scad + fit checks
./build.sh enclosure   # STLs in enclosure/stl/ + interference checks
./build.sh viewer      # ../viewer/mini28.json (+ the mini28 entry of ../viewer/dims.json)
./build.sh pcb         # place -> DSN -> Freerouting -> SES -> pours/stitching/BOM -> DRC (mainboard/drc.rpt)
./build.sh fab         # assembly values, DRC, Gerbers + drill (zip), position file, assembly drawings, renders
(cd mainboard && ../../tools/kicad.sh python3 gen_mainboard.py retrofit && ../../tools/kicad.sh python3 gen_mainboard.py bom)
                       # part data (values, C-numbers, footprint graphics), stackup and BOM onto the routed board, no re-route
BLEEP_BATT="42.3,51.4,4.9" ./build.sh enclosure    # a measured cell: the back plate's rails and stops follow it
```

KiCad 10 and Freerouting 2.5 run in Docker through `../tools/kicad.sh` and `../tools/freerouting.sh`. Heavy steps take a shared lock (`HEAVY_LOCK`, default `$TMPDIR/bleep-heavy.lock`). OpenSCAD: `OPENSCAD=` or `~/.local/bin/openscad`. `./build.sh pcb` retries the routing until DRC is clean (§8 risk 10); a re-route changes the copper, so for part changes that keep the pads use `retrofit` instead.

## 1. Mechanics

### Stack (z from the outer back face)

| z (mm) | |
|---|---|
| 0 – 1.5 | back plate (flat, held by 2 snap fingers at the top + 2 × M2×8 at the bottom, heads ≈ 1.4 mm below the back face in 3.0 mm counterbores) |
| 1.5 – 6.3 | the 4.8 mm cell (under the keys, y 104.9–155.9); the module, motor, PicoBlade header + plug, Q4/R24 elsewhere |
| 6.3 – 6.8 | swell gap over the cell |
| 6.8 – 7.6 | **the deck** (printed, 0.8 mm) under the board in the key area, from just below the module to the bottom edge; the board screws' washers and heads hang under it (to z 4.9), beside the cell |
| 7.6 – 8.4 | main board, 0.8 mm |
| 8.4 – 10.2 | under the LCD (**1.8 mm**): J1/J2 (FH34SRJ, 1.0 mm), both FPC runs, the touch tail's controller section (≤ 1.5 mm, chip down) with the main FPC (0.15) on it, foam pads; parts there ≤ 1.45 mm |
| 10.2 – 13.0 | LCD + backlight (2.8) |
| 13.0 – 14.5 | CTP glass (1.5) |
| 14.5 – 15.3 | front frame over the glass (0.8) |

H = 10.5 + cell thickness (9.7 + the 0.8 mm deck). Under the screen the board's underside carries only the motor and a few pillars now; the plate is 6.1 mm below the board there.

### Panel and the two FPC folds (from the drawings in `../docs/mini28/`)

- The module (LCD + white frame, 50.20 × 69.30) is wider than the glass (49.50 × 69.20, tops flush), so the glass pocket is sized on the module. The panel's top edge starts 0.53 mm past the IR LEDs' backs.
- **Display FPC**: 50 pins, 26.70 mm long, contacts on the FRONT face → after folding back under the LCD they face the board (bottom contact). Pin 1 stays on the right. Its parts strip (≤ 1.0 mm, rigid) is 3.7–5.6 mm out, on the back face.
- **Touch tail**: 6 pins, 37.90 mm long, centre 19.25 mm left of the panel centre, contacts on the BACK face → after the fold they face **up** (top contact). It runs outside the main FPC in the bend and lowest under the LCD. Its rigid FT6206 section (12 mm wide, 7.8–16.9 mm along the tail, ≤ 1.5 mm) ends up under the panel's bottom-left corner, chip down: the board keeps that area free of parts, and it sets the 1.8 mm gap.
- **Fold model** (redone after the audit): the radii follow the real landing heights. The tail lands on its own 1.5 mm controller section (bend r 1.65); the main FPC lands on the tail there (r 1.18). Both run 1.0 mm straight out of the panel first, so the main FPC's parts strip ends inside its bend and the tail's controller section starts after its bend, flat under the panel (fit-checked). J1 sits at (27.20, 57.49), J2 at (7.95, 47.78), each **2.0 mm** closer to the fold than the model needs (`FPC_SLACK`). The slack errs long on purpose: a short FPC is fatal (only a new board fixes it), a long one just bulges the fold (≈ 2.8 mm of room before the row under the screen). 2.0 mm covers the main FPC's rigid parts strip (3.7–5.6 mm out) being forced into a chord across the bend instead of the modelled arc (≈ 1.2 mm more FPC, ≈ 1.5 mm for the touch tail that wraps it) plus the drawings' ±0.5 mm length tolerance (fit-checked, as is the bulge of a long FPC). **Do the fold mock-up before ordering the boards if you can (§4 step 7).**
- **Hirose FH34SRJ** (both connectors): 1.0 mm high, contacts top **and** bottom, 0.3 mm FPC (both tails are 0.30 ± 0.03), back-flip lid. Contact 1 is at the footprint's +x end and the FPC enters from +y (from the fold), so **FPC pin n lands on pad n** for both (v1's XUNPU was 51 − n). Lands 0.3 × 0.8 as Hirose recommends; **stencil apertures 0.25 × 0.65** (Hirose's metal mask) to keep the 0.5 mm pitch free of bridges.

### Board support, cell and screws

- The board clears the walls by **0.6 mm** all round (PETG shrinks 0.2–0.5 % over 165 mm; set the slicer's XY shrink compensation for PETG anyway). Its side and top edges lie over the back plate's locating lip, and under the screen the **rails stand on the lip's footprint** (x 2.15–3.25 from each wall), carrying 0.85 mm of board edge; four Ø3 **pillars** carry the board's middle there (x 27.2 y 22 / 48, (40, 66), (20, 76)), now that no cell lies under the screen. The screws locate the board; the rails and pillars only stop it bowing.
- **The cell** (`BATT` = 42 × 51 × 4.8) lies **under the keys**, centred across (x 6.2–48.2), from y 104.89 to 155.89. It is located by **two short stops at each end** on the plate (0.8 mm thick, 1.5 mm tall, chamfered tops on the cell side): at the top (lead) end at x 8.2–12.2 and 38.2–42.2 (the leads pass between or over them), at the bottom near the corners at x 8.0–12.0 and 42.4–46.4 (they run into the bosses' standoffs); and at its sides by the **deck rails** (below), 0.7 mm from it all round. The bottom stops sit where a cell pushed down **and** into a side rail still keeps **0.3 mm** from the plate's lip in the 20 mm corners (fit-checked; a centred cell keeps 1.25 mm). Bay 43.4 × 52.4 mm: cells up to **42.2 × 51.2 mm** with 0.6 mm play per side. Margins for a centred cell: 3.4 mm to the USB-C joints' room, 3.1 mm from its top end to the module's pocket, 3.7 mm from the plug. **Buy and measure one cell before printing the back plate** and rebuild with `BLEEP_BATT="w,l,t"` (the rails, stops and the bay follow; a thicker cell also raises the whole stack). The bottom end stays put, so a longer cell grows towards J4: up to **≈ 52.7 mm** long before the 2 mm lead-bend room to the plug runs out (fit check); for a wider cell the bottom stops move up by themselves to keep the corners' 0.3 mm.
- **The deck** (`stl/deck.stl`, 0.8 mm PETG, printed flat, 4 layers at 100 % infill, with a brim; check that it lies flat): a plate between the cell and the board from just below the module (y 101.78) to the board's bottom edge, with an arm up the left edge to the top-left board screw (y 86.5). It **backs the board directly** under every key switch (the switches used to have pillars on the back plate, where the cell now lies; UP and PWR sit over the soldered module, which backs them). It is cut away round the USB-C joints and the reset pads, **notched out of the antenna keep-out's tail** (x ≥ 44.5, y < 105.2: no PETG under the antenna), and has a Ø2.4 hole for each of the five screws. It is **held by the three board screws** (their heads bear on it through a washer), so it stays with the board when the back plate comes off; with the plate on, the plate's **deck rails** (x 2.15–5.5 from y 102, and 48.9–52.25 from y 105.73, i.e. 1 mm below the antenna keep-out, down to the bottom stops) press up under its side edges and the plate's two standoffs under its bottom corners. The cell keeps its 0.5 mm swell gap under it. **The cell comes out without touching the deck**: remove the plate, unplug J4, lift the cell off the deck.
- Board → front shell: **3 × M2×4 with a DIN 125 M2 washer (Ø5 × 0.3) under each head**, through the deck and the board into heat-set inserts in the posts (from the underside, before the back plate goes on): one left of the D-pad (6.3, 90.03), above the cell, and two below the cell's bottom end (16.8 / 37.6, 159.43), between the bottom bosses and the USB-C joints. The washer spreads the clamp over the PETG deck (the Ø3.3 head alone would bear on a 0.45 mm ring round the Ø2.4 hole and creep). A board screw's head hangs 1.9 mm under the deck (washer + head), into the cell's height, so none can sit over the cell; the two lower heads stay 0.8 mm below the bottom stops' faces (y 156.59), so even a pushed-down cell never reaches them. 2.1 mm of each screw engages its insert.
- Back plate: two snap fingers (0.15 mm interference, ≈ 1.8 % strain) into grooves in the top wall, and **2 × M2×8** through the deck and the board into the bottom bosses (moved 1.0 mm out, to 10.5 mm from the sides, for the lower board screws), heads in **3.0 mm** counterbores (the 1.6 mm head ends ≈ 1.4 mm below the back face): the 1.2 mm the stack grew went into the counterbore, so the screws engage their inserts 2.6 mm, as before. Removing those two screws exposes the cell and its plug (EU Battery Regulation Art. 11).
- **Battery connector J4** (Molex PicoBlade 53261-0271, 3.4 mm): underside, at (18.5, 92.0), **left of the module, above the cell's top (lead) end**, **opening facing down** (towards the cell and the USB-C end): the mated plug's wires run straight on to the cell, under the deck's top edge (the plug ends 0.6 mm short of the deck and 3.7 mm short of the cell). Pin 1 = BAT+ ("+" on the silk), the right-hand pin seen from the front. **Q4 + R24** sit just above J4: Q4's drain straight above pin 1, R24 upright above Q4's gate, its GND end running left and down to J4's left tab (so the module's left-side pins keep their escape room).
- **Charger U5** (TP4056, up to ≈ 1 W): on the top side **left of the module, between the UP and LEFT switches** (19.5, 96.0), with R16 (PROG) and C11 (VBUS) left of it and C12 (VBAT) below its BAT pin; its PROG/GND/VCC/BAT pins and the passives' GND ends are joined by hand (vias for C11's and C12's GND). Its body ends 6.4 mm above the cell's top end (fit check: ≥ 5 mm) and 18 mm below the panel; its exposed pad's four thermal vias land on the underside in the plug's free zone below J4, not over the cell. (It sat in the right strip until the 2026-10-13 audit, partly over the cell's edge: §12.)
- **Motor**: on the underside **under the screen's lower left** (centre 10.9, 66.0), its wire pads below it; the DRV2605L's outputs run straight up the left strip to them.
- **Reset pads** (EN/GND, TP3/TP4): in the strip right of the cell (x 50.8, y 107.18 / 110.18), below the antenna keep-out, under a **2.2 × 6.4 mm slot** through the back plate and its right deck rail, which keeps a **0.8 mm wall** between the slot and the cell (the deck is notched there): reachable with the plate on, 7.6 mm deep. Short them with an insulated probe or plastic tweezers. The **TX pad** TP5 stays just above the module (42.2, 80.28), reached with the plate off.

### Row under the screen, mic port, keys

- Sensor window (IR receiver + light sensor, v1's translucent PETG pill) in line with the left key column.
- **Microphone port** in the middle (x 27.2): a Ø1.0 hole through the front face (drill it Ø1.0 after printing: vertical FDM holes this small come out ~0.85), continued as a **printed tube** (OD 2.8, bore 1.2, 0.8 mm wall) that **necks to OD 2.2 for its last 0.5 mm** and stops 0.7 mm above the mic's lid (the MSM261DHT006 is 1.05 mm tall). A **foam ring OD 2.2 / ID 1.4** (punched from the 1 mm foam tape, compressed to 0.7) seals the tube to the lid. The mic's port (Ø0.65) is 1.42 mm from the package edge at its pad-7 end, centred across (datasheet `../docs/MSM261DHT006_MEMSensing_datasheet_DS-042_v1.1.pdf`, p.9, checked against the pad numbering: the top view's pin-1 mark is at pad 1, the port at the opposite, pad-7 end; pinout and pad positions are the same as the MSM261DHP006 it replaced, whose port is Ø0.325 at 1.22). The footprint is rotated 180°, so **the port lies 0.58 mm from the package centre towards the keys** (x 27.2, y 82.68); the ring stays 0.3 mm inside the package edge and the port 0.28 mm inside the tube's bore. Acoustic path 5.2 mm (quarter-wave ≈ 16 kHz, above the speech band).
- **PWR key**: the cap is centred on the right key column (x 41.2); the switch sits 1.33 mm inboard (x 39.87) so the 0.6 mm VSYS rail can climb between it and the antenna keep-out, and the cap's stem is offset onto it.
- **Caps**: every stem stops **0.15 mm** above its switch's actuator (designed clearance; tune with `fit_test_keys` if a key feels loose or pre-pressed). The OK cap's flange has a 45° seat (prints face-down without a flat overhang), and the D-pad ring's inner bottom edge is chamfered to match, so a pressed ring doesn't push the OK (checked: `ok_vs_ring`).

- FPC slack ≥ the worst-case chord-shaped bend + the length tolerance (2.0 ≥ 1.5 + 0.5); an FPC that long bulges the fold by half its excess and still clears the row under the screen by ≥ 0.2 mm

- pillars under the screen clear of the motor, J4, Q4, the TX pad and the rails; board screw heads clear of the rails and standoffs; posts and bosses ≥ 0.4 mm apart (their pads' courtyards); posts 0.3 mm clear of every cap's flange

## 2. Main board

`mainboard/gen_mainboard.py` (adapted from v1's) places everything from `layout.py`, draws the power copper and a few sensitive nets by hand, exports to Freerouting, imports the routes, pours GND on both layers (stitching any cut-off pieces) and writes the BOM. JLCPCB rules: 0.13 mm clearance, 0.15 mm tracks, 0.3/0.6 mm vias, 0.4 mm to the edge; VBAT/BAT_RAW/VSYS 0.5–0.6 mm, other power 0.3 mm; no vias in SMD pads (the TP4056's exposed pad has 4 thermal vias on purpose, as v1). Silk: every text ≥ 1.0 mm high with a 0.15 mm stroke and ≥ 0.15 mm from pads, enforced as DRC rules.

GND stitching: a row of vias at 1.5 mm pitch along the antenna keep-out's top and bottom edges, wherever both pours are solid (on the final board: 5 along the top edge, just beyond the VSYS rail that runs 0.8 mm outside it; 6 along the bottom edge, x ≈ 43.5–51, between the keep-out and the rail; the left edge lies over the module; see §10) and a 6 mm grid wherever both pours are solid, never on or next to an SMD pad (`fab` checks that no via touches an SMD pad; the only exception is the TP4056's intended thermal vias).

Drawn by hand (fixed copper for the router): the VSYS rail (load-sharing pair → right edge → keep-out's left edge → LDO; on over the keep-out and up the right edge to the backlight resistors and the IR LEDs), the LDO and its 3V3 run into the module, EN, the module GND ties, the motor wires (bottom layer, straight up the left strip to the motor under the screen), the USB D+/D− pair (from the module's corner straight down the bottom layer, 0.55 mm apart, and v1's crossover under the connector), the crystal (pins → vias → crystal/caps/bias, symmetric), the mic's escapes through its GND ring, J4's pin 1 → Q4 → R24 and J4's GND pin and tabs, VBAT from Q4's source (bottom layer, between J4's pin 1 and tab) to the charger U5 and C12, the fuel gauge's VBAT sense line (bottom layer under the screen), U5's PROG/GND/VCC/BAT connections and its passives, U10's D+ pins to D+'s via, the EN line out to the reset pads in the right strip, and the TX pad.

**Antenna keep-out**: no copper on either layer from x 44.5 to the edge, y 79.7–104.7 (3.5 mm past the module's sides, 1.5 mm before the antenna section). It's a KiCad rule area, so the DRC enforces it. The module's antenna end is flush with the board edge.

Every key is on an RTC GPIO. Every GPIO is used. Boot states (ESP32-S3 datasheet pin table): IO41 (MTDI) resets with **no** pull, so R14 keeps the IR receiver off; IO44 (U0RXD) resets with a weak pull-up, which is harmless on the mic's DATA (high-Z whenever CLK isn't running, and R26 holds CLK low). I²C: FT6206 0x38, DRV2605L 0x5A, MMA8452Q 0x1C (SA0 low), LTR-303ALS 0x29, MAX17048 0x36: no clashes.

### Display FPC wiring (ER-TFT028A2-4 datasheet §4.1, ILI9341 4-wire 8-bit serial interface II)

IM3..IM0 = **1110** (pin 6 IM0 = GND; 7, 8, 9 = 3V3). Pin 1 LEDK → backlight MOSFET; 2–5 LEDA1–4, each through its own **56 Ω** from VSYS; 10 RESET → DISP_RST; 11–14 (VSYNC, HSYNC, DOTCLK, DE) and 15–32 (DB17–DB0) → GND; 33 SDO open; 34 SDI ← MOSI; 35 RD → 3V3; 36 WRX(D/CX) ← DC; 37 D/CX(SCL) ← SCK; 38 CS ← CS; 39 TE open; 40, 41 VDDI and 42 VCI → 3V3 (1 µF at J1); 43, 48–50 GND; 44–47 (resistive touch) open. Touch tail: 1 GND, 2 SDA, 3 SCL, 4 /RESET (DISP_RST), 5 /INT (IO14), 6 VDD (3V3, 100 nF at J2).

### Power path

USB-C VBUS (SMF5.0A TVS D4 + USBLC6-2SC6 U10 at the connector) → TP4056 (**500 mA**, R16 2.4 k) → VBAT. Load sharing as v1: on USB, VSYS = VBUS through D3 and Q3 is off; off USB, R18 (**10 k**) pulls Q3's gate low and the cell feeds VSYS through Q3. **Reverse-battery FET Q4** (AO3401A) sits between J4's BAT+ (BAT_RAW) and VBAT, on the underside right beside J4 (with R24; BAT_RAW never leaves that corner): drain to the cell, source to VBAT, gate to GND through R24 10 k. With the cell the right way round its body diode lifts the source, Vgs ≈ −VBAT turns it fully on (≈ 50 mΩ, 25 mV at 0.5 A) and it conducts both ways (charge and discharge); a reversed plug leaves it off and nothing on the board sees the negative voltage. The **fuel gauge** (MAX17048) measures its VDD pin; on this part CELL is "not internally connected" (datasheet pin table), so CELL and VDD are tied together to VBAT, as in its typical circuit. VBAT is behind Q4, so VCELL reads a few mV low: ≈ 25 mV at 0.5 A through Q4, ≈ 30–40 mV with the connector and leads. Fine for state of charge; a reversed cell never reaches the gauge.

## 4. Assembly

JLCPCB places and reflows **every top-side part** (Economic PCBA, top only, 255 ± 5 °C; files in `mainboard/fab/jlc/`, see §5). You solder only the **underside** by iron: the ESP32 module (with the EPAD-holes trick), J4, Q4, R24 and the motor's wires, plus the USB-C shell legs if JLC didn't do them. No stencil, paste or hotplate is needed. (Building a board entirely by hand: Appendix A.)

### Tools and materials

- Fine-tip iron + a broad chisel tip (EPAD holes, J4 tabs), no-clean flux, solder wick, tweezers, loupe, multimeter. A hot-air station only for rework (never on the mic's lid).
- PicoBlade crimper (Engineer PA-09 class), a stripper for 26–30 AWG, heat-shrink.
- Heat-set insert tip for M2 (or a spare iron tip), M2 screwdriver.
- Hollow punches Ø1.4 and Ø2.2 mm (leather punches), or a sharp Ø1.5/Ø2.0 pair, for the mic gasket ring; scissors for the foam pads.
- Drills Ø1.0 (mic hole), Ø1.5 / Ø3.0 (sensor window holes, if tight), Ø3.0 (insert holes, if tight); clear glue (UV resin or a drop of thin CA) for the windows.
- Filament: PETG in the body colour (shell, back plate, deck, caps), PETG in a second colour (key labels; a two-material printer for caps + labels), translucent blue PETG (windows).
- Kapton tape, 1 mm double-sided foam tape (10 mm wide).

### Steps

1. **Prints.** Print the parts (orientations as in the STLs; slicer XY shrink compensation on). The **deck** at 100 % infill with a brim (and elephant-foot compensation); check it lies flat on glass, and check the plate's deck rails with a straight edge (their tops carry the deck: a proud rail bows the key area towards the caps, a short one leaves it soft). Print `fit_test_keys` first and try a few caps on switches; if a key is pre-pressed or loose, adjust `STEM_CLR` in `layout.py`. Press the **5 heat-set inserts** (3 posts, 2 bottom bosses) in at ≈ 230 °C, **flush or a hair below** the post tops (an insert standing proud lifts the board and pre-presses the switches; check with a straight edge). Drill the mic hole Ø1.0 through. Glue the windows: the IR window from the inside (flange in its recess), the sensor window from the front (pegs down the holes), one small drop each.
2. **Check JLC's boards.** Under a loupe: J1/J2 (0.5 mm pitch) and U3 (MSOP-10) for bridges, U4/U7/U8 (QFN/DFN) sitting flat, D1/D2 lenses towards the board's top edge, D3/D4 cathode bars by the silk line, R17 empty (not fitted). Fix a bridge with flux and wick. Never blow hot air at the mic's lid (U9) and don't wash the board.
3. **Underside, iron.** Lay the board top side down in a **cradle** (the printed front shell, or a foam bed) so the tall top parts (IR receiver 4 mm, USB-C, LEDs) don't rock it.
   - **ESP32 module (U1)**: seen from the underside, its **antenna end goes to the LEFT edge** (the silk "<- antenna" points there; castellation 1 is marked "1" with a dot). Tack two corners, then drag-solder the castellations. Solder its centre GND pad from the **top**: feed solder into the four 1.0 mm holes (between Y1 and U2) until each one fills, with a hot, broad tip.
   - **J4 PicoBlade**: left of the module (seen from below: right of it), **opening towards the USB-C end**, pins towards the module, on its silk outline. Tabs first, then the two pins.
   - **Q4 (AO3401A), R24 (10 k)** just above J4 (towards the screen end): flux, tack one pad, solder the rest (SOT-23 and 0603 are easy by iron).
   - **USB-C shell legs**: if JLC didn't hand-solder them (order remark), solder the four slots from the underside.
   - **Motor**: stick it by its adhesive on its circle under the screen's lower left (seen from below: lower right), wires to the pads below it (red to the pad marked "+", which is MOT_P / the DRV2605L's OUT+).
4. **Bring-up before the panel.** Plug in USB-C: 3V3 at C2. Flash over USB (esptool resets it into download mode by itself; if a broken firmware prevents that, hold OK and short the RESET pads TP3/TP4 through the back plate's slot with an **insulated probe or plastic tweezers**, not bare metal ones: the cell lies 0.8 mm beyond the slot's wall). The ROM boot log appears on TP5 (TX, 115200 baud) with the back plate off.
5. **Battery plug.** Hold the cell on the board's underside over its silk outline (under the keys), its lead end towards the module, route its leads (they leave its top end, in the middle or at a corner) to J4's opening, and **cut them to the measured path + 10 mm** (≈ 15–30 mm from the middle of the PCM end, **up to ≈ 40 mm** if they leave its far (right-hand) corner; spare lead would coil over the pouch). One lead at a time (never cut both together): strip 1.5 mm, crimp a PicoBlade contact (50079-8100 for 26–28 AWG, 50058-8000 for 28–32 AWG), click it into the 51021-0200 housing with **red in the cavity that lines up with J4's "+"**. **Check the polarity with a meter before the first plug-in.** Without USB a swapped plug does nothing (Q4 stays off), but **with USB plugged in a reversed cell turns Q4 on**: the charger then pushes ≈ 50 mA backwards into the cell and Q4 dissipates ≈ 0.25 W until the cell's protection trips, while the remote runs normally from USB.
6. **Foam and gasket.** Stick foam pads (two layers of 1 mm foam tape; three for more preload) on the three FOAM boxes on the board. Put a strip of Kapton on the LCD's metal back over the area of U4/C14/U8 (the 0805 C14 leaves ≈ 0.35 mm under the panel). Punch the mic ring (OD 2.2 / ID 1.4) from the foam tape and stick it centred on the end of the printed mic tube.
7. **Fold mock-up — do it before ordering the boards if you possibly can** (buy the panel first). An FPC that turns out **short** can't be fixed: the panel can't move towards the top end (it is 0.53 mm from the IR LEDs), so only a new board with J1/J2 moved would help. An FPC that is too **long** is harmless: the fold has ≈ 2.8 mm of room before the row under the screen, and J1/J2 already sit 2 mm closer to the fold than the model needs. The mock-up: print a 1:1 slab of the board outline with J1's and J2's mouth lines (J1 mouth at y 59.31, J2 at 49.60 from the board's top end, see `layout.py`) and a 1.8 mm-high spacer where the panel goes; lay the panel on it as in step 8, fold once over a ≈ 1.2 mm mandrel at the necks (a drill bit), **never crease**, and mark where both FPC ends land. Each end must reach at least to its connector's fully-home line, 2.9 mm inside the mouth (J1 y 56.44, J2 y 46.73); a few mm more is fine. Don't refold the real panel repeatedly.
8. **Panel onto the board ("open book", outside the shell, folded once).**
   1. Board top side up on the bench, foam pads on (step 6).
   2. Lay the panel **face down** on a ≥ 5 mm spacer over the board's key area (the IR receiver is 4 mm tall), its FPC edge towards the board's top end and about 24 mm from J1's mouth (≈ y 83 from the board's top end), so both FPCs run straight and untwisted into J1/J2 (the panel lies entirely on the key side of the fold line).
   3. Flip both ZIF lids up, slide the 50-pin FPC into **J1** (contacts towards the board) and the touch tail into **J2** (contacts up, stiffener down), fully home, close the lids.
   4. Swing the **panel** over its bottom edge, once, onto the foam: both FPCs make one U-bend. Align its top edge 0.5 mm below the IR LEDs' backs and centre it across (it overhangs the board by 0.3 mm each side), then tack it with two Kapton strips over the board's side edges.
9. **Into the shell.** Front shell face down on a soft cloth. Put the caps in: **the D-pad ring first, then the OK through it**, then the others (they rest on their seats). Turn the board-and-panel unit over (glass down) and lower it straight in: the glass into its pocket, the board onto the three posts (the drop path is clear: the panel cavity is a plain prism 0.3 mm wider than the panel, with no ribs, and the board drops straight onto the posts; checked on sections through the shell by the final audit). Lay the **deck** on the board's underside (the three screw holes line it up; its arm goes up the left edge seen from the front, the right edge seen from now), and screw the board down **through the deck** with 3 × M2×4, a **washer under each head** (snug, not hard: the deck is PETG). Put a small Kapton square over the two lower screw heads (they sit 0.8 mm below the cell's bottom stops).
10. **Cell and back plate.** The board's underside, with the deck on it, now faces up. Lay the cell on the deck over the keys (no Kapton needed: the deck insulates it from the board), lead end towards the module, its bottom end at the bottom bosses; plug it into J4 (push by the housing; pull it out by the housing too, never by the wires; a Kapton pull loop helps) and lay the leads flat (they cross the deck's top edge and pass over the plate's 1.5 mm stops). Optionally a 0.1–0.2 mm soft foam dot on the cell against rattle (no thicker: the swell gap is 0.5 mm). Hook the back plate's top fingers into the top wall, swing it down (its rails drop in beside the cell, its stops at the cell's top end), then fit 2 × M2×8. To replace the cell later: remove the 2 screws, lift the plate, unplug J4, lift the cell off the deck (the deck stays screwed to the board).

## 6. Firmware notes

- **Display**: ILI9341 over SPI (MOSI IO35, SCK IO36, CS IO37, DC IO38), 240 × 320, up to 40 MHz in practice. SDO is open (never read). DISP_RST (IO39) resets **both** the panel and the touch controller: hold it low **≥ 5 ms** (FocalTech parts want ms, the ILI9341 10 µs), then wait 120 ms before the ILI9341 and ≥ 300 ms before talking to the FT6206. Resetting the touch later resets the panel too.
- **Touch**: FT6206 at 0x38 on the shared I²C (IO47/48). /INT on IO14 (RTC) for wake-on-touch: use its monitor mode in sleep.
- **Backlight — HARD duty cap on USB.** IO46 PWM drives the cathode MOSFET; each anode has 56 Ω from VSYS. Worst case on USB: VBUS 5.25 V − 0.3 V (D3) = 4.95 V on VSYS, a low-Vf LED at 3.0 V → (4.95 − 3.0) / 56 = **35 mA per LED** at 100 % duty, against the panel's **20 mA per LED maximum** (80 mA for the four). Typical on USB: (4.7 − 3.2) / 56 = 27 mA. So the HAL must **clamp the duty to ≤ 55 %** (35 × 0.55 = 19 mA average per LED) **whenever USB may be present**, in the LEDC driver itself, so no UI path, crash or boot screen can exceed it. There's no VBUS-sense pin, and CHRG/STDBY both stay high with USB but no cell, a tripped PCM or a reversed cell, so treat USB as present when CHRG or STDBY is low **or** the gauge reads VCELL ≥ 4.15 V (VSYS then comes from USB), and allow more than 55 % only when CHRG and STDBY are high and the gauge shows the cell discharging (CRATE < 0) below 4.15 V. On battery: worst case at a full 4.2 V cell is (4.2 − 3.0) / 56 = 21 mA, so clamp to ≤ 93 % there; typical is ≈ 71 mA total at 4.2 V, dimming as the cell drains: per LED ≈ 18 mA at 4.2 V, 9 mA at 3.7 V, 6 mA at 3.55 V (and 27 mA on USB). PWM can only lower the brightness, so "scale the duty with VCELL" can keep it even only down to the level the weakest cell voltage gives: expect the screen to be about 3 × dimmer near empty than when full. At boot IO46 is held low (100 k), so the backlight is off until the firmware starts it.
- **Microphone**: I²S PDM RX, **CLK IO40, DATA IO44**, left slot (L/R low). The clock must stay in standard mode, **1.1–4.8 MHz**: for 16 kHz speech use the 128× down-sampling (`I2S_PDM_DSR_16S`, 2.048 MHz); 64× (DSR_8S) at 16 kHz gives 1.024 MHz, inside the mic's dead band (check the ratios against the IDF version). R26 holds CLK low when it isn't driven, so the mic sleeps (< 1 µA) in deep sleep; stop the clock (or set IO40 to input) before sleeping.
- **IR receiver**: drive **IO41** high to power it, wait ~100 ms, capture IO42 with RMT, then drive IO41 low again. IO41 resets with no pull and R14 (100 k) holds it low, so the receiver is off at boot and in deep sleep (IO41 is not an RTC pin; no hold needed, but don't enable its pull-up).
- **32 kHz crystal**: `CONFIG_RTC_CLK_SRC_EXT_CRYS`. Watch the boot log for "32 kHz XTAL not found". The 8.2 pF caps assume ≈ 2 pF stray; if the RTC calibration shows a large offset, try 6.8 pF ([C1679](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C1679.html)).
- **Charger**: 500 mA. CHRG (IO12) and STDBY (IO3) are open drain with no external pull-ups: enable the internal pull-ups (`rtc_gpio_pullup_en` in deep sleep). CHRG low = charging, STDBY low = full, both high = no USB. The TP4056 dissipates up to **(5.0 − 3.0) V × 0.5 A = 1.0 W** at the start of constant-current charging on a drained cell (≈ 0.75 W at 3.5 V); inside the closed shell it will reach its thermal regulation and reduce the current for a while. That's expected.
- **USB unplug**: R18 is 10 k, so Q3 takes over within ~0.1 s. Still worth testing on the first board: pull the cable at ≈ 3.5 V during Wi-Fi TX.
- **Wi-Fi brown-out limit**: the cell feeds the LDO through ≈ 0.2–0.25 Ω (PCM + J4, Q4, the VBAT run, Q3), and the ME6211 drops ≈ 0.35–0.45 V at Wi-Fi TX currents, so 3V3 sags below 3.0 V during TX once the cell is under ≈ 3.5–3.55 V. Cut off Wi-Fi (or shut down) at ≈ 3.55 V under load, set the ESP32's brown-out detector deliberately, and measure on the first board.
- **Reversed cell**: if CHRG reads low and VCELL < 2.5 V, show "check battery polarity" (see §4 step 5).
- **Swollen cell**: the cell lies under the keys now, so a cell swollen past its 0.5 mm gap pushes the deck and board up under the switches (stems 0.15 mm above the actuators): keys start to press themselves. If a key reads held for minutes (or several at once) on an older cell, show "check the battery for swelling" and treat it as a replace-the-cell warning.
- **Haptics**: the motor sits under the screen now, ≈ 24 mm from the nearest screw, so its buzz reaches the hand through the board, rails and pillars rather than a nearby post. Tune the DRV2605L's effect strength on the first build. The accelerometer is 37 mm from it (lift-to-wake shouldn't see the pulses, but ignore ACC_INT while an effect plays to be safe).
- **Charging temperature**: the TP4056 has no enable pin here and TEMP is grounded, so the firmware can't limit or stop charging; the PROG resistor (R16) sets the current. See §8 risk 22.
- **IR LEDs**: IO45, 33 % carrier duty (≈ 125 mA peaks at 4.2 V, ≈ 155 mA on USB). R2/R3 are 1206 for long repeat trains.
- **Strapping**: IO0 = OK key (download mode when held at reset); IO45 and IO46 are low at boot through their 100 k gate pull-downs; IO3 is only a strap with a JTAG eFuse.
- **Debug**: IO43/U0TXD goes to TP5 (underside, above the module). Use the USB-Serial/JTAG console normally. IO44 is the mic now, so don't enable UART0 RX.
- **Fuel gauge**: MAX17048 0x36; VCELL is VBAT behind Q4 (its VDD pin): a few mV low, ≈ 25–40 mV at 0.5 A of charge or load. Read it at light load, or add I × ≈ 70 mΩ if you want the cell's own voltage. Haptics, accelerometer as v1 (DRV2605L 0x5A with an ERM, MMA8452Q 0x1C INT1 on IO2). The accelerometer sits under the panel, far from the motor.

## 8. Open risks

1. **The panel source**: buy ER-TFT028A2-4 + ER-TPC028-4.1 from buydisplay.com; the AliExpress listing's photos show the ER-TFTM028-4 breakout module.
2. **The fold**: FPC lengths (26.7 / 37.9 ± 0.5), the rigid sections' positions (main parts strip 3.7–5.6, tail controller 7.8–16.9, ≤ 1.5 mm) and FH34SRJ's insertion depth (≈ 2.9 mm, not dimensioned by Hirose) come partly from drawing graphics. 2.0 mm of slack (erring long) covers a chord-shaped bend and the tolerance; the mock-up (§4 step 7), ideally before ordering boards, is the real check. A short FPC needs a new board; a long one is harmless. If the controller section is thicker than 1.5 mm, the 1.8 mm gap is too small.
3. **484251 cell**: PCM length and lead position unverified (measure, then rebuild the plate), and Ampul's shipping of loose cells to Cyprus is unverified. 1500 mAh is 30 % less than the 444666 it replaced (§12).
4. **Board fit**: 0.6 mm per side and 0.85 mm on the rails; with heavy PETG shrink the post pitch (y 90–159) can still pull the board ±0.3 mm in its 2.2 mm holes. Use the slicer's shrink compensation. The deck shrinks too: its screw holes are Ø2.4 (0.2 mm play on M2).
5. **Backlight on USB** depends on the firmware's hard 55 % clamp (§6), including the VCELL ≥ 4.15 V rule for USB without a working cell. Without it, the LEDs run at up to 35 mA each. On battery the screen dims ≈ 3 × from full to empty.
6. **VCI = 3.3 V**: the top of the ILI9341's 2.5–3.3 V range (abs. max 4.6), as on many ILI9341 boards.
7. **Microphone**: the gasket ring is hand-punched and seals on a 0.4 mm-wide ring of lid; the 5.2 mm tube is longer than the 2–3 mm rule of thumb (resonance should still sit above 8 kHz). The MSM261DHT006 trades 4 dB of SNR for Economic PCBA (60 against the DHP006's 64 dB(A)); it is an LGA (reflow only; JLC places it) with ≈ 2.8 k in stock at JLC. Its MSL isn't listed. Its DC offset (4 % FS) needs the usual high-pass. The order remark (no washing or ultrasonic cleaning, don't cover the port) matters.
8. **Crystal**: the cap values depend on stray capacitance (tune with 6.8 pF). Each crystal net has one via (module underneath); only slow, static lines (keys, CHRG, 3V3) pass near them (K_DOWN's escape runs 0.26 mm beside the XTAL_P via), no fast signals.
9. **Antenna**: the keep-out complies, but the module's antenna ends 0.6 mm from the PETG wall (v1 ≈ 1.9; Espressif suggests much more), and the nearest metal is now the top-left post's insert/screw (6.3, 90.03), left of the module, far from the antenna end (the post below the antenna is gone). Compare RSSI with v1 on the first build.
10. **Routing**: Freerouting routed it; its optimiser pass is skipped (it counts the hand-drawn connections as open), so tracks are not length-optimised. The board is near Freerouting's capacity. A run is repeatable for the same .dsn and settings, but KiCad's DSN export orders things differently each time, so `build.sh pcb` retries with a fresh .dsn and cycling router settings (fanout off, pull-tight accuracy), up to 15 times, until DRC is clean; that can take several attempts.
11. Thermal vias sit in the TP4056's exposed pad, as on v1; its paste is one pane between the via columns (JLC builds its stencil from our paste layer), so little paste reaches them. The LDO's GND via is under the module (tented).
12. USB-C: the HRO footprint's rear shell slots are 1.2 mm long where SHOU HAN's drawing asks for 1.4 (v1 has the same pairing): check that the rear legs drop in on the first board.
13. Top-side GND pads join the pours solid (reflow, not iron). Hand rework on them needs a hot, broad tip.
14. **Reversed cell with USB plugged in**: Q4 conducts, ≈ 50 mA reverse charge, ≈ 0.25 W in Q4, until the cell's PCM trips. Check polarity with a meter before the first plug-in.
15. **Wi-Fi brown-out** below ≈ 3.5–3.55 V cell under load (§6).
16. **LTR-303ALS-01** ([C364577](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C364577.html)) is NRND at LCSC (≈ 15 k in stock): fine for now, check before each order.
17. **Reflow ratings vs Economic's fixed 255 ± 5 °C peak** (Standard PCBA runs 240 ± 5 °C). Every top part is rated to 260 °C peak (datasheets: IRM-H638T, LTR-303ALS, VSMB2948SL, USB-C, the mic, MMA8452Q, MAX17048, DRV2605L, TP4056, USBLC6, SMF5.0A, the AO340x and the passives) **except the Hirose FH34SRJ J1/J2: 250 °C max** (FH34 / FH34SRJ catalogues, temperature profile: 250 °C max, 60 s above 230 °C; their flip actuator is polyamide). JLC lists them as Economic parts and reflows them routinely, but 250–260 °C is up to 10 °C over Hirose's rating. Options: accept it (check every actuator flips and latches; a warped one is a ZIF swap by hot air at ≤ 250 °C or iron), order Standard PCBA instead (240 ± 5 °C, within every rating; ≈ $55 more for 5 boards), or leave J1/J2 off JLC's BOM and solder them yourself (350 °C iron, 5 s, 0.5 mm drag soldering under the lid). No published reflow data for the TS-1187A switches (LCP body, steel cover: fine in practice), the ME6211 or the B5819W (generic molded SOT-23-5 / SOD-123). The FC-135 crystal is rated 260 °C, 3 reflows, but its standard profile allows 35 s above 220 °C; JLC's longer time above liquidus falls under Epson's optional J-STD-020 profile, with a few ppm more frequency shift (the ESP32 calibrates its 32 kHz clock against the main crystal). Cycle limits (IRM-H638T and LTR-303ALS: 2; mic and crystal: 3) allow JLC's single pass plus one rework.
18. **HASL instead of ENIG**: flatness for the 0.5 mm-pitch parts (J1/J2, U3, U4, U8) is now JLC's process (Economic accepts ≥ 0.4 mm pitch on HASL). The underside hand soldering (module castellations and EPAD holes, J4, Q4, R24) is easier on HASL than on ENIG. The logo's LED dots come out silver.
19. **Economic DFM review**: JLC can still refuse a part for Economic. The likeliest is the mic (an LGA; JLC lists the DHT006 as Economic with no X-ray flag, unlike the DHP006). If they do: Standard top-only, or remove U9 and solder it with a preheater from below (never hot air on its port).
20. **Key feel over the deck** (2026-10-13): the key switches press on the board backed by the 0.8 mm deck, which spans the 43.4 mm between the plate's deck rails over a 0.5 mm air gap to the cell; the old design had a rigid pillar under every switch. Estimated deflection under a key press (≈ 1.6 N, board + deck as a strip) is ≈ 0.15 mm at the middle, well inside the swell gap, but the keys may feel slightly softer in the middle of the key area. If they do: print the deck at 1.0–1.2 mm (`DECK_T`, the shell grows by the same) or add a soft 0.3 mm foam strip on the cell under the D-pad.
21. **Cell leads**: the 484251's lead position on its PCM end is unverified (middle or a corner); the plug zone and the top stops allow either, but measure the cell before printing the plate. A cell longer than ≈ 52.7 mm (PCM outside L) needs J4's lead room re-checked (`BLEEP_BATT`, §1).
22. **Charger heat next to the cell** (2026-10-13 audit MA1): U5 dissipates up to ≈ 1.0 W at the start of a 500 mA charge (≈ 0.75 W typical) and nothing measures the cell's temperature (TEMP is grounded). U5 now sits left of the module, 6.4 mm above the cell's top end (it was partly over the cell), with its thermal vias into the bottom pour below J4. **First-build test:** a thermocouple on the pouch's top end (nearest U5) and one in the middle, charging at 500 mA from ≈ 3.0 V with the shell closed at room temperature; the pouch should stay below ≈ 40 °C (Li-Po charge limit 45 °C). If it doesn't: R16 2.4 k → 3.3 k (≈ 360 mA, same footprint; a JLC BOM value swap).
23. **Swelling**: a swollen cell now pushes the key area (§6 "Swollen cell"): keys pressing themselves are the early warning. Replace the cell.
24. **Key feel vs print tolerance**: the board's key area rests on the deck, which rests on the plate's rails with zero designed interference; a rail printed 0.1–0.2 mm proud bows the area towards the caps (stems 0.15 mm above the actuators), a short one leaves it soft. Check the rails with a straight edge and the deck for flatness (§4 step 1); if needed, sand the rail tops or add a 0.2 mm foam strip on them for a defined preload. Three board screws clamp the deck (one above, two below the cell), so the span is shorter than mini24's.
25. **Haptic strength**: the motor moved under the screen (§6 "Haptics"); if it feels weak on the first build, add a pillar on the plate under its can (3.4 mm of room) so it is clamped to the back plate.

## Appendix A. Building a board fully by hand

Only if you ever build a board without JLC's assembly (e.g. from bare PCBs): order a frameless stencil with the boards (top only, 0.12 mm; 0.10 mm is kinder to the 0.5 mm pitch; custom size ≈ 100 × 230 mm, remark "top paste layer only") and buy the top-side parts at LCSC too (the BOM's top-side C-numbers are JLC basic parts; if one has no LCSC stock, take an equivalent: 100 nF X7R 0603, 1 µF X5R 0603, 10 µF X5R 0805 25 V, 10 k 1 % 0603).

Extra tools: hotplate **≥ 200 × 200 mm** or a T-962-class reflow oven (or a 100 × 100 mm plate with the two-zone method below), hot-air station, lead-free paste, a spatula/card, two spare boards to frame the stencil.

**Which pads get paste**: the stencil has an aperture on every top-side SMD pad **except** R17 (not fitted), the module's four EPAD holes (soldered from the top by iron), and **J3's four shell legs** (plated slots: no aperture, iron them from the underside). J1/J2 get Hirose's reduced 0.25 × 0.65 apertures, U3 (MSOP-10, 0.5 pitch) 0.25-wide ones, and the TP4056's exposed pad one 0.9 × 2.0 pane between its thermal-via columns. The underside has no paste: it's all iron work.

Then, in place of §4 step 2:

**Top side: paste and reflow.**
   - Tape the board between two spare boards, align the stencil, print the paste once over the whole board.
   - Place every top part from `mini28-assembly-top.pdf` (refs on the silk, values on the drawing). Orientation: pin-1 dots for every IC, J1/J2, U10; D1/D2 lens towards the board's top edge (arrows), cathode on the pad marked K; D3 and D4 cathode bar by the silk line; SOT-23s by their pad pattern.
   - **Reflow on a ≥ 200 mm plate or in the oven**, in one pass.
   - **With a 100 × 100 mm plate, two zones:** first set the top end (y 0–105: the IR LEDs, everything under the panel, J1/J2, the sensor row, the mic, the LDO and crystal; let the parts near the plate's edge reflow again in the second pass if they didn't flow) on the plate, the rest overhanging onto a level support of the same height (a cold metal block); reflow. Then slide the board so the key end (charger, USB-C, U10/D4, the switches) is on the plate and the finished top end overhangs; reflow. **The mic (U9, y ≈ 82) must be well inside the first zone** so it sees one reflow only. The switches near y 100 may reflow twice; that's fine.
   - Check J1/J2 (0.5 mm), U3 (0.5 mm), U10 with a loupe; wick bridges. Fix any part with hot air **except the mic**: never blow hot air at its lid (rework: Kapton over the port, low flow from the side). No washing or ultrasonic cleaning afterwards.
